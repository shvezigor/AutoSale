# Decision method

Keep an evidence ledger: observation, source/date, segment, confidence and limitation. A sales claim, interview comment, working prototype and measured production result answer different questions. Do not collapse them into one confidence score.

An assumption is a belief needed for the proposed outcome. Identify the riskiest one and choose the cheapest authorized way to test it. A manual workflow or fictional prototype may be sufficient before a provider integration. A prototype does not validate operational reliability; an interview does not measure conversion.

For prioritization, honor the user's criteria. If no criteria exist, propose a qualitative comparison using supported user impact, evidence, effort range, dependency and reversibility. Mark unknowns explicitly and identify the discovery step that would resolve them. A confidence-adjusted number with invented inputs is still invented.

For metrics, specify the eligible unit and entry cohort, event boundaries and full follow-up window. Example: unique eligible import sessions that produce a persisted approved catalogue within 24 hours / unique eligible sessions that start import. Repeated clicks are events, not extra users. Exclude test traffic consistently; never remove failures just to improve the result.

Choose a decision threshold proportionate to the test: qualitative evidence may justify another prototype, not a company-wide rollout. Predefine guardrails for correctness, data exposure, cost and support burden. Do not promise a numeric business benefit without measured support.
